Ada Lovelace

Imagining what a computer could do.

A vision beyond arithmetic

In 1843, Ada Lovelace published an English translation of Luigi Menabrea’s account of Charles Babbage’s proposed Analytical Engine, adding extensive notes of her own. Her notes described how the machine could carry out sequences of operations, including a procedure involving Bernoulli numbers.

She also considered possibilities beyond ordinary calculation. If other subjects could be represented through suitable symbols and rules, she suggested, a machine might work with them too. Music was one example she discussed.

That idea makes her work interesting to computer science students: computing can involve more than doing arithmetic faster. It can open up different ways to represent and manipulate information. Her writing offers a useful historical starting point for asking who imagines the purposes of technology and whose contributions are remembered.
