Implicit Bias

A short introduction to implicit bias, bias tests, and intersectionality.

What is implicit bias?

Implicit bias involves automatic associations about people that we may not notice. These associations can differ from our conscious beliefs and may affect how we judge others.

Implicit Bias Tests

The Implicit Association Test (IAT) compares how quickly people sort words or pictures when different categories share a response key.

Gender-Career IAT

This test pairs male and female names with words about careers and family. The pairings switch during the activity to compare the strength of these associations.

Race IAT

This test uses Black and White faces alongside positive and negative words. It compares response times when the category pairings change.

A result can encourage reflection, but it is not a complete judgment of someone’s character. Project Implicit explains that a single score is unlikely to predict one person’s behavior on a specific occasion. Group-level patterns can still be meaningful.

Intersectionality

Intersectionality, developed by Kimberlé Crenshaw, examines how systems of inequality overlap. Race, gender, and class do not shape people’s experiences separately. For example, Black women can face discrimination that is missed when race and gender are considered one at a time.

Why it matters

Noticing bias is a starting point. In a group project, rotating coding and presentation roles can give everyone a chance instead of relying on assumptions about their abilities. Listening to different experiences can also reveal barriers that are easy to miss.
